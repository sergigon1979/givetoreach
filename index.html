Give to Reach

I get more first-time email than I can read. A small tool I built sets it aside and replies for me with one request: a $2 gift to GiveDirectly, as a sign that your email was meant for me.

Sergio González

CRO at Talka. Before that, Stripe, Twitter and Google. LinkedIn

How it works

  1. An email from an address that is new to my inbox is set aside, unread. No person reads it. A model scores it, and the score never decides whether I read it; the gift does.
  2. You get one note in the same thread, with a link made for your email.
  3. You give $2 to GiveDirectly on Every.org, a US nonprofit donation platform. Every.org sends you the receipt.
  4. Every.org tells Give to Reach the gift went through. Your email comes back to my inbox, and I answer it myself within three business days: a real answer, yes or no, from me.
Your gift goes to GiveDirectly through Every.org. None of it comes to me. At checkout, Every.org offers to add a tip for itself; that is optional.

If this note is not for you

A client, or someone I know? The note reached you by mistake, and I am sorry. Reply to it with one line and I will read it myself. A pitch in that reply gets the same answer: the link.

Would rather not? No hard feelings. Your email stays set aside, unread, so my silence is not an answer.

Writing again? A second email from the same address gets no second note; it is set aside too. One gift covers you: after it, your later emails come straight to my inbox.

Why $2, and why GiveDirectly

The amount is small on purpose. It is not a fee; it is a sign that you meant to write to me and not to a list. GiveDirectly gives cash to families in extreme poverty, and its results have been tested in independent trials.

What it keeps about your email

The note has no images and no tracking pixels. An AI model scores your email for relevance. What is kept, on a small server I run: a category, a few numbers, short excerpts with names, companies and links replaced, the code in the link, and a one-way hash of your address. Not the text. Nothing is ever deleted, and results are published only as pooled counts. The privacy policy has the full detail, in a section written for senders.

Questions

Is this real?
Yes. One reply, in your own thread, to an email you sent me. The link opens GiveDirectly's page on Every.org, not a page of mine. If you would rather not use the link, give on every.org yourself and reply to the note with the date. The tool writes only to people who wrote to me first, and never twice to the same address.
Will you actually reply?
Yes. The tool holds me to it: when your gift lands, your email is starred in my inbox with a deadline, and I answer within three business days.
Is the money really going to charity?
Yes. Every.org takes the gift and sends the receipt. I never see your card and never touch the money.
I gave and heard nothing.
Reply to the note with the date of your gift. I read those myself.
