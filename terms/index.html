Give to Reach is a Gmail add-on built and run by Sergio González. It is free, and none of the money a sender gives reaches him or anyone else involved. By installing it you agree to the following. The privacy policy is part of these terms.

1. What it does in your name

  • It runs inside your own Google account, as you, once an hour. It reads mail from senders you have never dealt with, has each one classified by an AI model (see below), and, for the ones the model calls a cold pitch, moves them out of your inbox under a visible label and sends one reply in that thread, from your address and in your name, asking the sender to give $2 to GiveDirectly to have the email put back. Anything else a first-time sender writes you stays in your inbox with a label saying what it looked like.
  • Nothing is deleted. You can review, restore, mark a sender as known, or stop the whole thing at any time from the add-on's card, and by uninstalling it.
  • Every email from a first-time sender is read by an AI model. The model's answer is what decides whether an email is set aside, so this is how the add-on works rather than a setting; installing it is agreeing to it. Your own name, addresses and company are replaced before the text leaves your account; the sender's text goes as written and is discarded after it is read. The privacy policy names the provider, says exactly what is sent and what comes back, and is updated and dated before the provider ever changes.
  • Nothing is read, held, scored or answered until you have filled in the four profile fields on the settings card. Until then the add-on does nothing to your mail.

The last of those is how the add-on works, so it is worth settling before you install.

3. The reply promise is yours

  • The automated reply promises the sender an answer within a number of business days (three by default; you set it) once their gift has landed. That promise is made in your name and it is yours to keep. The add-on restores the email, stars it, shows you a countdown and nags you, but it cannot answer for you.
  • You approve the wording of the reply, and you are responsible for it as for any email sent from your address. The reply carries your name and your company; it never carries a postal address.
  • Before installing, remove any Gmail filter or other rule that archives unknown senders; it would work against the add-on and could hide a restored email from you.

5. Scoring by an AI model

  • Scoring is optional and switched on by you. When it is on, the text of a held pitch is sent from your own account to Anthropic and scored against a fixed rubric, as described in the privacy policy.
  • The scores, categories and excerpts are the opinions of a model about a piece of writing, not facts about a person or a company. They decide only whether an automated reply goes out; anything the model does not read as a cold pitch stays held for you to look at, and nobody is replied to. Do not use them for any decision about a person beyond answering their email.
